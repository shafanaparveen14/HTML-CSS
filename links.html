The Greenhouse Effect: Earth's Natural Blanket and the Challenge of Climate Change The greenhouse effect is a natural, essential phenomenon that regulates Earth’s temperature, making it suitable for life. Similar to a botanical greenhouse, the Earth’s atmosphere acts as a protective layer, allowing sunlight to pass through while trapping heat re-radiated from the surface. Without this process, the Earth's average surface temperature would be approximately -18°C (−0.4°F), rather than the current comfortable average of roughly 14°C (57°F). Wikipedia Wikipedia +4 How It Works When sunlight reaches Earth, the surface absorbs the energy and re-emits it as infrared radiation (heat). Greenhouse gases (GHGs) in the atmosphere—principally water vapor ( ), carbon dioxide ( ), methane ( ), and nitrous oxide ( )—act like a blanket. They absorb this infrared radiation and radiate it back toward the surface, preventing it from immediately escaping into space. NASA Science NASA Science +4 The Enhancement Effect (Global Warming) While the greenhouse effect is natural, human activities have intensified it over the last 150 years. Burning fossil fuels (coal, oil, and natural gas) for electricity, transportation, and industry releases vast amounts of additional and other GHGs into the atmosphere. Deforestation also contributes, as trees that once absorbed are removed, adding to the imbalance. This increased concentration of gases traps more heat than necessary, leading to a rise in global temperatures known as global warming.
